# 1. Visual intent

The ECF educational illustration system should feel like:

**premium homeowner building-science education**

It should not feel like:
- generic contractor stock imagery;
- fake customer-project evidence;
- architecture visualization;
- children's educational art;
- engineering-manual diagrams;
- alarmist mold/water-damage marketing.

The system should help ordinary homeowners understand:
- where a symptom may originate;
- why multiple building systems can contribute;
- what conditions are worth inspecting;
- why spray foam is one part of a larger building-envelope system.

---

# 2. Core art direction

## Background

Preferred:
- warm off-white;
- soft sand / warm neutral;
- very light parchment.

Avoid:
- pure clinical white unless the composition requires it;
- dark dramatic backgrounds for homeowner education;
- high-contrast horror/damage framing.

## Architecture

Use:
- realistic Lowcountry residential proportions;
- raised/crawl-space homes where appropriate;
- familiar wood framing / sheathing;
- plausible roof, wall, attic and crawl-space geometry;
- clean cutaway / editorial illustration treatment.

Avoid:
- luxury-architecture hero rendering;
- church/commercial-campus imagery unless the asset specifically calls for commercial;
- impossible framing;
- decorative complexity that hides the lesson.

## Spray foam

Accepted visual language:
- cream / warm ivory / light beige;
- realistic irregular cellular expansion;
- enough texture to read as foam without looking like food, clouds, or stone;
- neutral surface tone.

Good/expected condition:
- continuous;
- bonded-looking;
- consistent enough to establish a visual baseline;
- no claim of code compliance based on appearance alone.

Needs-inspection condition may include:
- pull-away;
- visible gap / void;
- missed transition;
- irregular / cracked / friable-looking neutral surface;
- uneven application.

Avoid:
- black/gray mold-like speckling;
- fake rot;
- fluorescent chemical color;
- red failure overlays;
- unsafe flue / ignition-clearance ambiguity unless the lesson specifically requires it.

---

# 3. Color language

## Moisture / humid air

Use:
- desaturated blue;
- light blue-gray;
- restrained blue arrows/droplets.

Meaning:
- moisture movement;
- humid-air pathway;
- condensation / water context.

Do not automatically equate blue indicators with:
- mold;
- foam failure;
- contamination.

## Heat

Use:
- restrained orange / terracotta;
- warm amber;
- simple directional heat arrows.

Meaning:
- heat gain;
- hot attic;
- thermal movement.

## Inspection emphasis

Use:
- restrained orange/amber;
- small accent marks;
- subtle callout emphasis.

Avoid:
- alarmist bright red;
- giant X marks;
- "GOOD/BAD" baked-in labels.

## Healthy / expected condition

Optional:
- restrained muted green;
- use sparingly.

The website layer may provide clearer state labels than the artwork itself.

---

# 4. Line / detail treatment

Preferred:
- medium-fine architectural edges;
- clean framing outlines;
- soft but legible material texture;
- restrained sketch/editorial quality;
- enough detail for credibility;
- enough simplification for mobile comprehension.

Avoid:
- hyper-detailed CAD;
- cartoon outlines;
- photoreal CGI;
- watercolor looseness that obscures assemblies.

---

# 5. Text and label policy

Default rule:

**Do not bake substantial explanatory copy into AI artwork.**

Website HTML/CSS should own:
- exact labels;
- explanatory copy;
- alt text;
- mobile typography;
- CTA text;
- accessibility;
- responsive positioning;
- future wording revisions.

Artwork may include:
- arrows;
- droplets;
- heat indicators;
- subtle emphasis marks;
- simple iconographic cues.

Avoid baked-in:
- paragraphs;
- technical values;
- code references;
- price ranges;
- safety instructions;
- diagnosis labels;
- marketing claims.

---

# 6. Composition rules

## Desktop

Preferred:
- 16:9 or nearby editorial aspect ratio;
- clear main subject;
- safe empty zones for HTML/CSS callouts;
- avoid important information tight to edges;
- maintain one dominant lesson per image.

## Mobile

Do not assume the desktop image can simply be center-cropped.

For complex diagrams:
- create a dedicated portrait/mobile derivative;
- simplify callouts;
- enlarge the most important visual relationships;
- remove nonessential decorative context;
- preserve the lesson at phone width.

# 10. Image truth boundary

AI-generated illustrations are educational assets.

They must not be presented as:
- actual ECF customer property;
- actual failed installation;
- inspection evidence;
- real before/after proof;
- a documented ECF job;
- a specific homeowner's mold/moisture condition.

Real ECF project photography remains the proof layer.

Generated visuals should be described or positioned as:
- illustration;
- educational diagram;
- example condition;
- conceptual cutaway.

---

# 11. Production master requirements

Current accepted calibration references are 1672 × 941 PNGs.

They are sufficient for style calibration but should not be treated as final source masters.

Before public production use:
- regenerate or upscale accepted assets to true 2K-or-higher master resolution;
- preserve lossless PNG masters in the ECF Drive visual library;
- generate optimized web derivatives separately;
- consider AVIF/WebP for production delivery;
- produce dedicated mobile derivatives for complex compositions;
- retain clear filenames and version suffixes;
- never overwrite the historical calibration evidence without versioning.

# 13. Review gate for future images

Every new generated asset should be checked against:

1. Does it match A1/A2-v2/A3 in palette and realism?
2. Is the building geometry plausible?
3. Is foam shown consistently?
4. Does blue mean moisture/air without implying mold?
5. Does orange mean heat/inspection without alarmism?
6. Can a homeowner understand the main lesson without reading a paragraph?
7. Can website labels be added without covering the subject?
8. Will it still work on mobile or does it need a dedicated derivative?
9. Does it accidentally imply a capability ECF has not confirmed?
10. Could it be mistaken for real customer evidence?

If any answer is problematic, revise before adding it to the accepted visual library.
