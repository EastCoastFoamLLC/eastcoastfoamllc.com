# Visual system principles

## Preferred style

- warm Lowcountry residential feel;
- clean technical illustration rather than textbook engineering art;
- simple labels;
- limited information per visual;
- mobile readable;
- no sensational mold imagery;
- no horror/doom framing;
- no fake measurement numbers;
- no unverified code-compliance claims;
- no implied diagnostic equipment unless ECF really uses it.

## Color / visual direction

Concept only:
- warm off-white / light sand background;
- deep charcoal / navy text;
- green/teal accent for "expected / good";
- amber accent for "inspect / check";
- red reserved only for active-water / immediate stop conditions;
- simple cutaway geometry;
- subtle Lowcountry cues, not beach-house clichés.

# BRIEF 01 — Lowcountry house symptom cutaway

## Goal

Help a homeowner understand that several common comfort/moisture symptoms can come from different parts of the building.

## Composition

Simple side cutaway of a one- or two-story Lowcountry house.

Callouts:
1. hot upstairs room;
2. very hot attic;
3. attic air leak / gap;
4. HVAC duct in attic;
5. roof/water intrusion;
6. wall condensation;
7. musty crawl space;
8. ground vapor/moisture;
9. cold/damp floor;
10. exterior humidity.

## Message

**The symptom tells you where to look — not automatically what product to buy.**

Secondary line:

**Insulation, air sealing, moisture control, ducts, roofing and drainage can all affect comfort.**

## Best format

Custom illustration.

Do not use stock photography.

## Suggested public locations

- Start Here / What are you noticing?
- Mold/moisture education
- homepage education teaser

## Alt-text concept

"Illustrated Lowcountry home showing common comfort and moisture symptoms in the attic, walls, ducts, rooms and crawl space."

# BRIEF 02 — Good spray foam vs needs inspection

## Goal

Show homeowners visible conditions that justify an inspection without teaching them to self-diagnose code compliance.

## Panel A — expected visual characteristics

Illustrate:
- continuous coverage;
- bonded to substrate;
- no obvious missed cavities;
- neat transition around framing.

Label:

**Looks continuous and bonded**

Not:
"Code compliant" or "correct thickness."

## Panel B — pull-away

Illustrate foam separated from a rafter/stud.

Label:

**Pull-away / separation — worth inspecting**

## Panel C — void / missed transition

Illustrate:
- visible substrate;
- unfoamed corner;
- gap around framing transition.

Label:

**Gap or void — air-seal continuity may be incomplete**

## Panel D — questionable surface condition

Optional later panel:
- irregular / friable-looking material.

Label only after Casey confirms he sees this enough to be useful.

## Footer disclaimer

**Illustrative examples only. Required thickness, foam type and assembly details vary by project.**

## Best format

Custom illustration plus, later, real ECF photos beside it.

# BRIEF 03 — Four moisture paths

## Goal

Make the mold/moisture page understandable in less than 20 seconds.

## Four quadrants

### 1. Bulk water

Icon:
roof rain / leak.

Examples:
- roof;
- flashing;
- plumbing;
- flood.

Message:
**Stop active water first.**

### 2. Ground / crawl-space moisture

Icon:
soil + vapor arrows.

Examples:
- wet soil;
- standing water;
- drainage;
- missing/incomplete ground vapor control.

### 3. Humid-air leakage

Icon:
humid outdoor air moving through gaps.

Examples:
- air leaks;
- crawl/attic pathways;
- unsealed penetrations.

### 4. Condensation

Icon:
humid air meeting cold duct/surface.

Message:
**Warm humid air + cold surface can create liquid water.**

## Center message

**Insulation is one part of moisture control.**

## Source anchors

EPA mold/moisture
DOE / Building America

# BRIEF 04 — Conditioned attic: whole-system concept

## Goal

Explain why "spray foam on the roof deck" is not by itself the whole conditioned-attic strategy.

## Show

- roof covering / drainage plane;
- roof sheathing;
- spray-foam boundary;
- sealed/unvented attic;
- ducts/HVAC inside conditioned boundary;
- moisture removal / conditioning pathway;
- ceiling plane / living space;
- no open soffit/ridge vent arrows in the conditioned-attic example.

## Callouts

- **Air + thermal boundary**
- **Roof must still stay dry**
- **Moisture still needs a strategy**
- **Ducts may benefit from being inside the boundary**
- **Assembly details matter**

## Guardrail

Do not prescribe one exact dehumidification/duct strategy in the illustration.

# BRIEF 05 — Existing spray-foam inspection flow

## Goal

Turn the second-opinion/audit service into a clear, credible process.

## Proposed sequence — Casey confirmation required

1. **Tell us what you're noticing**
2. **Accessible visual inspection**
3. **Document visible conditions**
4. **Check relevant moisture / RH / thickness information**
5. **Explain what appears normal vs worth correcting**
6. **Recommend next step**

Possible next-step outcomes:
- monitor;
- isolated repair;
- add/complete missing coverage;
- correct water/moisture source first;
- remove/replace affected material;
- refer to roofing/HVAC/other specialist;
- no foam work recommended.

## Most important line

**An inspection should not assume the answer before we look.**

Only publish if Casey agrees with that operating philosophy.

# BRIEF 06 — Repair / add / remove / leave alone decision tree

## Goal

Help a homeowner understand that existing spray foam does not automatically require full removal.

## Flow

**What is the concern?**

→ visible isolated gap/pull-away  
→ inspect extent  
→ possible localized repair

→ widespread incomplete coverage  
→ inspect assembly + thickness  
→ possible corrective/additional work

→ active roof/plumbing/ground water  
→ stop  
→ correct water source first

→ persistent odor / sticky/friable/discolored material  
→ contractor/product/manufacturer review  
→ determine corrective/removal path

→ installation appears continuous but comfort remains poor  
→ look beyond foam  
→ ducts / HVAC / air leakage / windows / moisture strategy

→ no current problem / documentation review only  
→ monitor / retain records

## Disclaimer

**This is an education tool, not a remote diagnosis.**

# BRIEF 08 — Open-cell vs closed-cell, plain English

## Goal

Give a fast comparison without turning one product into a universal winner.

## Visual treatment

Two large material-profile cards.

Compare:
- feel/density;
- expansion;
- typical R-value tendency;
- vapor openness/retarder behavior;
- common application patterns;
- sound behavior;
- cost tendency;
- moisture/assembly considerations.

## Footer

**The right choice depends on the assembly, climate, code, product and project goals.**

Use sourced/current values only.

# BRIEF 10 — Before / during / after install

## Goal

Reduce uncertainty before a spray-foam job.

## Timeline

### Before
- assessment;
- scope;
- fix active leaks;
- prepare access;
- occupants/pets plan;
- product/re-entry instructions.

### During
- masking/protection;
- substrate/prep;
- application;
- quality checks.

### After
- cleanup;
- curing/ventilation according to product instructions;
- documentation/photos;
- re-entry per actual manufacturer/product guidance.

## Guardrail

Never hard-code universal "24-hour re-entry."

# Image quality gate

Reject an image if it:
- looks like generic AI construction art;
- shows unsafe PPE/practice;
- contains obviously impossible foam geometry;
- implies mold diagnosis;
- shows a tool ECF does not use;
- makes generated failure imagery look like a real ECF project;
- adds decorative clutter without teaching something;
- cannot be understood on a phone.

The goal is not to "add images."

The goal is to make building-envelope concepts easier to understand and ECF's real expertise easier to trust.
